# WebKit

Retest drives Playwright's WebKit build 2359 (WebKit 26.6) on macOS over the build's inspector pipe, through its own client. Playwright itself is never loaded. The [guide](../guide.md#webkit) says how to configure a WebKit target. This page lists where WebKit ends a shared case otherwise than Chrome, and why.

## Engine differences

Each item is what WebKit does, read on build 2359. The driver reports it as it is, or refuses by name, and no assertion was changed to hide it. The shared suites and the conformance cases hold every case one of them changes to exactly what WebKit gives, and a declaration that stops holding fails its case. Items 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are declared: the refused connection's message and details, the title in the goto and in the navigation, the move back after a given-up entry, the cell named by its text in conformance F8.1a, and the phone page. Every declaration is in `tests/integration/engine-differences.ts` and cites its item here by number and by the bold words it opens with. A unit test fails when a declaration cites an item this page does not hold. A declaration is allowed only for a case that passes on Chrome, so a failure Chrome is meant to have cannot be relabelled as an engine difference. Chrome declares nothing.

1. **A failed navigation is told in WebKit's words.** An address nobody answers fails as `not_actionable` with "Could not open http://127.0.0.1:61527/: Could not connect to the server.", where Chrome's says `net::ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED`. The details are exactly the address with its query removed and `errorText: 'Could not connect to the server.'`. The driver passes WebKit's text on. The shared cases assert that exact message and those details on WebKit. Assert WebKit's own text when checking a refused connection.
2. **A title keeps the control characters `document.title` keeps.** WebKit strips and collapses only ASCII whitespace, as HTML says; Chrome's `document.title` also reads each C0 control character as a space. A title with ESC reaches the parent as `\x9BBell tab \x1B[2J` on WebKit and `\x9BBell tab [2J` on Chrome. The parent cleans both to the same record. The title case of `browser-navigation.test.ts` asserts Chrome's raw text.
3. **WebKit's history stays at an entry it gave up.** A move back onto a response with no content fails at once, naming the address, and the page keeps its document. WebKit's history, though, now stands at the entry it gave up: the next `goto` drops the entries after it, so a later `goBack` returns to that entry, not to the page that stayed (probe: the next goto's page reads `history.length` 3, and the `goBack` after it returns to the entry given up; the same steps in `browser-navigation.test.ts`, which passes on Chrome, go back to the page that stayed). Restoring it would take another navigation, which Retest does not make on its own. In the case for a move back onto a response with no content, the `goto` after the given-up move succeeds, and the next `goBack` goes to the given-up entry, whose address answers with no content again, so it fails as the first did ("The page stayed on …/flip.") and the page keeps the document of that `goto`; on Chrome it returns to the page that stayed.
4. **Roles and names WebKit's tree computes its own way.** Read side by side with Chrome in `webkit-roles.test.ts`: 152 pages of element kinds, each looked up by all 82 roles without a name, plus named and label lookups and the select options. Where WebKit names an element its own way and the page itself says what Chrome names it, the driver names it as Chrome does, by the element's kind (`chromeRoleOf` in `roles.ts`): a one-option select is a combobox, a number field a spinbutton, an input whose `list` attribute names a `<datalist>` a combobox (unless it is a range or a colour; read from the attribute, since WebKit's own `list` is null for its date fields), a date, time, month or week field and an editable `<div>` or `<span>` with no role nothing a lookup asks for, a `<figcaption>` not a caption. Every other difference is a doubt the reading of the document reports (`accessibility-reading.ts`), and a lookup a doubt could change is refused by name, before any input goes: the role `generic`, always; `img` on a page with an `<img>` without `alt` WebKit leaves out, or an `<svg>` with no role; `option` without a name when the page holds a select's options, and with a name only when one of those options could carry it (its text, `label`, `aria-label`, `title` or `aria-labelledby` text); `group` with a select's option group or an `<hgroup>`; `emphasis` and `strong` with a shown `<em>` or `<strong>`; an option, list item or tree item outside its listbox, list or tree, and the role WebKit kept it as; an element whose role attribute WebKit overrode, both roles; every role on a page with an editable element other than a `<div>` or `<span>`; names of a cell, grid cell, column or row header or tooltip when one of them has no `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`; names of every role a `<label>` names when a hidden `<label>` labels a control; a table WebKit reads as a layout table. On that table, with the shown-label case: 12581 lookups, 12189 answered as Chrome answers, 392 refused by name, none answered differently. A cell named by `aria-label`, and a custom option beside a native select, are now answered.
Conformance F8.1a asserts `error`, class `unsupported`, at `toBeVisible` on the cell named Grace Hopper, with detail `role: 'cell'` and at once. Its literal message is "Could not look up getByRole('cell', { name: 'Grace Hopper' }): Chrome names a cell from its text, and WebKit's accessibility tree does not, so Retest refuses the lookup rather than find less on WebKit. Find it with getByText() or getByTestId(), or by its position with nth()." A cell with an explicit accessible name remains usable.

5. **A select of several options never passes through one nobody asked for.** WebKit's list chooses with Home, End, Shift and the arrows, and every key the page hears as a change. The plan starts from the first option asked for with Home when it is the list's first, or from the last with End when it is the list's last, and widens over the options asked for only; one option before them is passed only when the list already holds it alone. A set with a gap, or one away from both ends, is refused by name before any key is sent. `webkit-driver.test.ts` logs every `input` and `change` of such a list and holds each to the options asked for.
6. **No phone or touch screen.** A WebKit target takes a viewport, a pixel ratio and a user agent. A mobile layout or a touch screen is refused at setup as `unsupported` with exactly "Retest's WebKit driver cannot emulate a mobile layout or a touch screen. Give this WebKit target a viewport, or a screen with isMobile and touch false." The touch input path is not built for WebKit, so `tap` is refused too. The phone case of `browser-actions.test.ts` asserts that exact refusal. Use a desktop viewport; this does not verify a phone layout.
7. **Console and network cover the document and its frames.** Build 2359 renders a page's frames of every site in the page's own web process, so their console messages and requests reach the page's target; each request names its frame, and each console message its script, which WebKit's debugger names the context of (the page turns the debugger on with every pause off; measured: no cost to a page's script, and `debugger;` does not stop). The scope covers the document and frames of either site; workers are not followed, and a worker's request, which WebKit sends on the page's target under the worker's id, is left out. A message with no time of its own is counted unread rather than given the parent's clock.
8. **The helpers run outside the browser's process group.** Networking, GPU and web content processes are XPC services launchd starts, with parent pid 1 and groups of their own. The driver attributes each to its browser with `launchctl print pid/<browser pid>` while the browser runs, records it by pid, start time and command, and ends only what it recorded, after checking the record still matches. A home with a record file lets the next launch sweep what a killed Retest process left, and leave a live one alone. The sweep compares a recorded process with `sameProcessIdentity` from `src/shared/process-ownership.ts`: the same pid and start time, a command line that macOS reads as the process's kernel name in parentheses while it exits counted as unreadable rather than different, and two readable command lines that differ still refused (`webkit-sweep.test.ts`). Every other check of a recorded process goes through `OwnedProcessGroup`, which uses the same rule. A helper whose command line cannot be read when launchd first lists it is not recorded, so it is never signalled. A reading that fails is no proof either way: `processStartedAt` tells "no such process" (a whole `ps` reading without the pid) from "could not read" (it throws), and the sweep leaves a home whole, signalling nothing, when the Retest process that made it or any recorded process could not be read, and while any process has the pid of a Retest process whose own start was never read.
9. **A timeout is told only once its budget has passed.** A poll's last look waits all but a moment of its budget for the page to change, then reads. A timer can fire a fraction of a millisecond early by the clock, and a read that timed out at such a moment reached the caller before its deadline, which then took it as the failure of the check: S8 in one conformance run, and `toHaveURL` in one run of `webkit-lookup`, each ended `timeout` instead of `check_failed`. The WebKit page now waits out the rest of the budget before it answers a timeout. Measured with 200 such last looks per engine on one machine: before the change, WebKit answered 2 timeouts before the budget had passed and Chrome 1; after it, WebKit answered none and Chrome, unchanged, 3. Chrome's page has the same race. The same holds for a refusal at the end of an action's time: a failure that is a timeout, or that says it waited the whole budget (`details.waitedMs`), is told only once that budget has passed by the clock (`waitedWholeBudget` in `page.ts`), since the browser call that ended the last look can time out more than a millisecond early, while the deadline still reads time left. Seen in a conformance run: A12, a covered checkbox, refused after 499 of its 500 ms. `webkit-driver.test.ts` holds 100 refusals of a hidden button and 100 of a covered checkbox to their 60 ms budget; before the change, one of the hundred hidden-button refusals came at 59.753 ms.
10. **Looks at once take the page target in turn.** Every `DOM.getDocument` gives every node of the document a new id, so two looks once shared a half-cancelled document: 400 buttons were counted as 0, 100, 200 or 300, or a look threw. The document is now asked for once per world, each look holds its target's turn from its reading to making each element it found an object, a node the page removed is read again rather than left out, and a look that keeps losing its reading until its budget is spent fails as a named timeout, never as fewer elements.
