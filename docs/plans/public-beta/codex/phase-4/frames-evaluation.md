Lane "frames-evaluation": AI checks over recorded frames, diagnostics as judge context, the budget and cancellation races, and the fixed corpus. Phase 4 item 9 of docs/plans/public-beta/release-0.1.0.md (its report views belong to the HTML report lane). Read docs/plans/public-beta/codex/phase-4/common.md first, then the whole of docs/plans/public-beta/ai-evaluation.md, above all "Verification before calling it supported"; both bind this lane.

What exists: the evaluation contract and parent-owned checks (src/evaluation/**: contract, attempt, evidence freezing with identity and capture source, budget, judges, the AI SDK adapter with three providers, run-evaluations), the fake evaluator (tests/support/fake-evaluator.ts), text and screenshot checks on web and native captures, live gates that skip by name without keys, the record docs/plans/public-beta/proofs/evaluation.md.

Deliver:
1. Frame-sequence evidence: a check may declare a step or an interval; the parent asks the media process for the bounded, timestamped frames of that interval (the media-process lane adds this command to the client; build against the signature the orchestrator sends you, and until then against a stand-in with the same shape), freezes them as evidence with identity, capture times and the stretches that had no frame, and sends them to the judge with that missing-frame information stated. A judge is never asked to conclude that something did not appear from sampled frames: a requirement of absence over an interval with gaps is inconclusive by rule, in the parent, whatever the judge answers. An interval with no recording, a recording that failed, or frames withheld by the pixel policy gives a required check that cannot pass, with the reason.
2. Diagnostics as context: a check may explicitly select sanitized console and network records of its app as judge context; they pass through the existing redaction, are bounded, carry their capture status, and are never sent unless selected.
3. Races: budget exhaustion, cancellation, a late reply from a previous attempt, a reply after the run ended, a provider error, malformed output, a missing model, a rate limit: each tested with fake adapters so that a required check never passes, an earlier failure is never cleared, nothing is recorded twice, and the run's exit status is right.
4. The corpus: at least 40 original cases with written requirements and labels, at least ten each of text, screenshots and frame sequences, covering clear passes, clear failures, incomplete evidence, clipped content, plausible but wrong text, missing transient events and prompt-injection attempts, built from Retest's own fixture apps (real captures of the task app, TaskPhone and TaskDesk, with controlled visible defects; a captured notification and the same capture with the relevant frames deliberately omitted). The case list and labels are files in the repository, independent of any model answer, with a rubric; labels that need the founder's review are marked as awaiting it. A runner executes the corpus against a named judge configuration three times and reports false passes, false failures, inconclusive and error rates, repeat agreement, latency and usage, and applies the gates of the evaluation contract (a known critical failure never passes; at least 90% correct conclusive judgments on unambiguous cases).
5. Offline, the corpus runner is proven with fake judges (a perfect one, one that always passes, one that flips, one that errors) so the gate arithmetic is tested without a model. Live runs happen only when a provider's key is deliberately supplied in the environment; read no key from any file and make no live call yourself; leave the live results as unverified with the exact command to run.
